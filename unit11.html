Unit 11

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Christ could have chosen a perfect resurrected body free of all blemishes. However, He chose to keep the wounds in His hands, feet, and side as a symbol of His Atoning Sacrifice for us, assuring us that He has suffered the worst of all of us, yet He conquered it and lived, and through Him so can we."

Context Summary

Lesson 19 focused on the resurrected Christ's visit to the Lehite people in the Americas, following a period of destruction and divine judgment. The lesson examined His teachings to the surviving Nephites, His healing of the sick, His blessing of the little children, and His prayers on their behalf, as well as His instruction on prayer and the responsibilities of a covenant people.

Full Journal Submission

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 1

Record your answers to the following questions in your journal. Include specific examples to back up your conclusions: Why did Christ cause the destruction of these cities and their inhabitants? Who perished, and who survived? What are His purposes for all this devastation? How does Christ describe His own perspective on what has happened? How does He feel about all this death and destruction?

Christ caused the destruction for multiple reasons. For one, He did so as it fulfilled Old Testament prophecy. Secondly, He did so to encourage the “more righteous,” likely those that He knew had moldable hearts that were possible to change, to repent. It does not appear that Christ is delighting in the death of all of those people. It is more like He is devastated by their sins and sad that it had to come to this.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 2

Answer the following questions in your study journal: How does this comparison of Christ to a woman expand your understanding of His nature? What does the comparison to a persistent mother tell you about Christ?

Comparing Christ to a woman, such as a hen or a mother, is a powerful symbolism of His divine character. It describes Christ in much the same way that Luke does: caring and compassionate to the marginalized and in pain. It demonstrates Christ's mercy and continued outreach to us, with arms outstretched reaching for us.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 3

After watching the video above, think about the following: What do Jesus's body and His physical actions communicate to the people? What does His power to heal teach them (and you) about Himself? Record your thoughts in your journal.

The fact that Christ could choose an unblemished body but chose to keep His wounds in His hands, feet, and eyes is a powerful testament of how His Atonement was a desire to understand and have mercy on us, as it says in Alma 7. This communicates that He was one of us and that although He is divine, He had a mortal experience just like each of us. His power to heal teaches that He could get rid of those marks but He chooses to maintain them. It teaches of His longsuffering mercy with each of us.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 4

As you read, note in your journal everything that this account teaches about the resurrected Christ's feelings or emotions.

I love that healing them and blessing the little children does not appear to be part of Christ's original plan, although He likely knew full well that this would happen. However, He gives them the chance to feel the desire that He stays longer, internally pleading He remain. I love that Christ can feel their thoughts and have compassion for them and that it is then that He heals and blesses the children. Although Christ may have already been planning to do so, He demonstrates that our desire to remain close to Him leads to His presence and miracles beyond what we had even asked for, because He knows of our needs.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 5

After you watch the video with Elder Andersen, write a response in your journal. What stood out to you in his words? How have you seen the healing power of Christ evident in your life and in the lives of others? What questions about healing do you still have, and how can you find answers to your questions?

I have seen Christ heal my newborn sister in the hospital while she had meningitis and heal me when I had appendicitis that very same week. I saw Him help me with good people when I broke my arm that were angels to me, and help me have a positive perspective when I broke my arm two weeks later. I do wonder, like Elder Holland, why people don't always get healed, but I figure there are many reasons. I had a list of reasons I thought of and scriptures that applied, but there are many more reasons.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 6

As you read 3 Nephi 19:15-36, look for principles of prayer that you can learn both from observing Jesus's prayers and from what Jesus tells the Lehites about prayer. Write down some ideas in your journal. Are there any principles of prayer taught here in 3 Nephi 19 that you could use to make your own prayers more powerful, meaningful, or holy? Write down at least one goal that you can apply in your prayers.

What I noted about Jesus' prayer is that He is overwhelmingly concerned about others. I fear that sometimes I can be selfish or self-centered during my prayers. My goal is to pray for others more and for unity and peace in general rather than making my prayers so “personal” that I forget about those around me.

Christ's Postmortal Ministry, Prompt 7

In your journal, answer the following: What does it mean to you to be part of a “covenant people”? What responsibilities does that place upon you in your own situation?

To be a covenant person means that I follow Jesus, have chosen Him through baptism, and choose to keep those covenants. This gives me the responsibility to keep the commandments, serve others, share the gospel, and take His name upon me. Being a covenant people and staying true to that means Christ promises to save us.
