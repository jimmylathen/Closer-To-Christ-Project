Unit 9

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Oftentimes our intentions and motives behind our actions are just as important as our actions themselves. When you choose the right, think about why you are choosing to do so."

Context Summary

Lesson 15 studied Jesus as a master teacher through His sermons and personal conversations, including the Sermon on the Mount, the Bread of Life Sermon, and His conversations with Nicodemus and the woman at the well. Lesson 16 focused on the final twenty-four hours of Christ's mortal life, examining the Last Supper, His final discourse, the Garden of Gethsemane, and His trial before the Jewish authorities. Together, these lessons showed the Savior at His most instructive and His most vulnerable, inviting both deep reflection and renewed commitment.

Full Journal Submission

Sermons, Prompt 1

As you read these chapters, look for key teachings that touch your heart. What stands out to you personally?

A lot of what I read was very touching to me. It was amazing to see how bold Christ was and how He raised the standard. I loved the way that He helped people look internally and stopped hypocrites. Reading the Sermon, it is nearly impossible to push blame on others. I realized I need to take more responsibility and have my eye more single to God.

Sermons, Prompt 2

Now that you have studied these sermons from Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew, what stood out to you the most? Was there something in particular Jesus taught which touched your heart to prompt you to take some action?

The main thing that stood out to me was my intentions. I need to do what is right first and foremost. But I also need to be conscious as to why. Am I doing it so that others or even myself might perceive me a certain way? Am I doing it out of duty, obligation, habit, or culture? Or do I do the good that I do in order to love and serve God and my neighbor? Am I motivated by pride? Is my eye single to the Lord? I need to reevaluate what I treasure, what I value, and what I want, because I know my treasure will be that. I need to prioritize the Lord and put Him first rather than my own pleasure or desires.

Sermons, Prompt 3

Now that you have studied the Bread of Life Sermon, how might you apply what was taught (or what happened before or after the sermon) into your life today?

This sermon was very touching to me. I believe every Christian has come across something they have read, heard, or seen that confused them or made them question their testimony. In these moments it is difficult, especially when we ask the Lord and for His own reason and purpose He does not explain it clearly. The question is what will we do? Will we go away and walk no more with Him? If we see those we love leave because of these questions and confusions, will we follow or double down on our testimonies and try to help bring them back as emblems of faith?

Sermons, Prompt 4

In your scripture journal, please answer one of the following questions: Was there something in particular that Jesus taught Nicodemus or the woman at the well that touched your heart to prompt you to take action? How did the contrast between the two conversations make a difference in your mind as you read and studied these chapters?

What touched me was the way in which the woman at the well was so humble, unoffended, and ready to spread the good news. I felt that I needed to be humble and stand in the light of God, study Him, and never hide, because that light clears guilt and becomes hope and peace that comes from the Holy Spirit. I have a testimony and witness of that light. I want to immerse myself more with God. I loved the concept in these stories that Jesus reaches out to all. Although He originally commissioned His disciples only to teach Jews, He taught and loved everyone, preparing the future outreach to Samaritans and Gentiles. People can surprise us, so we must never judge and share the gospel with all.

The Last Supper and Gethsemane, Prompt 1

Which lesson, principle, or teaching from the Last Supper is most meaningful to you personally? Take a moment to write down how you could apply it in your life.

I think the most meaningful aspect of the Last Supper was pride. Much of what the disciples did was a reflection of their own subconscious pride. Whether it was asking who it was who would betray or Peter saying that all of them might deny Him but he never would. I love John's example of asking internally, “Is it I?” It reminds me of Christ's commission on the Sermon on the Mount for us to remove our beam before we worry about someone else's mote. I need to look inward critically rather than outward.

The Last Supper and Gethsemane, Prompt 2

Which lesson, principle, or teaching from the Savior's final discourse is most meaningful to you personally? Take a moment to write down how you could apply it in your life.

Honestly, the most meaningful thing I read was about the truthfulness of the unity of Christ, the Father, and the Holy Ghost as three separate beings. Many scriptures have led me to sometimes be confused by this, as some have seemed to show them together in some ways while most seem to support this separation. I believe these chapters show their unity and separation of entities better than any other. I felt a witness of the divine nature of the Godhead as I read this. I especially felt so when it said that they would be persecuted in Christ's name, which ties Him to the identity of Old Testament Jehovah, because they knew not who had sent Christ, having not known Elohim.

The Last Supper and Gethsemane, Prompt 3

Which lesson, principle, or teaching from the Savior's experience in Gethsemane is most meaningful to you personally? Take a moment to write down how you could apply it in your life.

The main lesson I was able to take away from the Garden of Gethsemane reading was the way in which Christ was humble and willing to submit to the will of the Father. The idea that Christ has a distinct will is fascinating to me and gives me comfort when I feel like obedience is difficult for me as my will and priorities are failing to align with God's. I know that like the Savior, I can draw on a deeper purpose and humility in not seeking my own peace or gratification, but trusting God, knowing He knows what's best for me and is the only way to true and everlasting joy.

The Last Supper and Gethsemane, Prompt 4

Based on our discussion in this module, why do you think that some of the Jewish authorities sought to kill Jesus Christ?

I think that the chief priests wanted to kill Jesus because He posed a threat to their authority, power, and wealth. Christ came in on a donkey and was said to be the Davidic king. The chief priests made lots of money from the temple and it was said that Christ would destroy the temple. They also thought He spoke blasphemy.
