Unit 5

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Differences between divine witnesses of Christ as our Savior are not contradictions. Rather, they are different perspectives that provide more information about distinct truths relating to Christ's grace and perfection, just like the artistic depictions of Him are not identical but equally valuable."

Context Summary

Lesson 9 explored how the four Gospels present distinct portraits of Jesus Christ, each emphasizing different aspects of His life, mission, and identity. Students examined different approaches to reading the Gospels, including harmonic, sequential, and synoptic reading, and analyzed the unique emphases of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. Understanding these differences helps readers recognize that each Gospel writer's perspective adds to a fuller, richer witness of the Savior.

Key Scriptures

  • Mark 1:1-11
  • Matthew 3:1-17
  • Luke 3:1-22
  • John 1:19-34
  • Mark 1:41; 3:5; 10:21; 14:33-36

Full Journal Submission

Portraits, Prompt 1

Compare and contrast reading the New Testament Gospels in a harmony, in sequence, or in a synopsis.

Reading the New Testament Gospels in a harmony, in sequence, or in a synopsis are several very different approaches. Harmony means looking at each story as teaching the same thing about Jesus, combining all into one conception of Him. Reading in sequence is reading cover to cover. Reading synoptically means reading story by story, bouncing back and forth between the Gospels to compare and contrast the accounts of Jesus.

Portraits, Prompt 2

Which approach to reading the Gospels have you primarily done thus far in your life?

I have mostly done a harmonic approach through seeing videos and portrayals of Jesus in that way.

Portraits, Prompt 3

For each verse you read, record the human experience Christ had or the emotion He felt.

Verse 1, Jesus felt pity. Number 2, He felt anger. Third, it appears He is sighing with frustration. The human experience in the fourth verse is suffering and facing contempt. The next verse mentions indignation. The sixth verse mentions love. The seventh verse acknowledges Christ's grievances, distress, agitation, and even pain or anxiety for what He was about to experience. The final verse mentions Christ feeling alone and forgotten by His Father. Each of these verses demonstrate Mark's unique recognition and description of Christ's humanistic experiences in a way that allows us to relate to Him and have faith that He can help us overcome all things.

Portraits, Prompt 4

What are the three themes of the Gospel of Matthew?

The three themes of the Gospel of Matthew are that he emphasizes the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy, he connects Christ with Moses, and lastly he connects Christ with David.

Portraits, Prompt 5

What are the three themes in the Gospel of Luke?

The three themes in the Gospel of Luke include Jesus as a universal Savior, the emphasis on women, and Jesus' ministry to the poor and the outcasts.

Portraits, Prompt 6

What similarities did you notice in each of these accounts? What differences did you notice?

I noticed that Mark's account did not mention the prophecy of Esaias in the same way as Matthew, who mentioned it as a fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy. I also noted Luke's was longest but less centered on Christ's actual baptism, but wrote many names of many people and said Judea, fitting the more universal Jesus concept.

Portraits, Prompt 7

What did you read in this paper that you didn't notice in your initial reading?

I didn't realize the way in which John had John the Baptist explicitly denying being the Christ. I also didn't notice Luke's reference to the poor and needy nor the reasoning behind his deep description of the place in time in which this occurred. Furthermore, I didn't notice the way in which Matthew and Luke had John explain the destruction Jesus would bring.
