Unit 7

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Christ is inextricably connected with both our spiritual and temporal joy in this life and the world to come. To approach Christ is to approach joy. Parables are designed to be interpreted many different ways according to our needs and spiritual maturity. For example, the Good Samaritan can mean everyone is our neighbor, but it can also mean love God, and that Christ considers everyone His neighbor. The Good Samaritan can be a Samaritan or a figure of Christ."

Context Summary

Lesson 12 explored the Savior's use of parables as a teaching method, examining several parables including the sower, the laborers in the vineyard, the unmerciful servant, and the Good Samaritan and Prodigal Son, and what they reveal about the Savior's character, priorities, and invitation to change. Lesson 13 studied the miracles of Jesus as evidence of His divine power and compassion, examining miracles from Matthew, John, and other Gospel accounts to understand what they teach about His identity, His love for individuals, and His role as the promised Messiah. Together, these lessons showed a Savior deeply committed to reaching people where they are, both through story and through action.

Full Journal Submission

Parables, Prompt 1

Please answer the following questions in your scripture journal: Where do you see your own heart in the parable of the sower? Which parts of this parable stood out to you? Can you see any particular ways you might apply what you learned from the parable?

In Luke's description of the parable of the sower, he describes the seeds in the rock as falling away not because of persecution but rather temptation after having believed. I have a strong testimony of Jesus and have had many spiritual experiences, but sometimes temptation has distracted me from bringing forth good fruits by leading me to distractions that preoccupy me with less important things. I also liked Luke's description of the seeds in the good ground and that he mentions patience in their good fruits. I think with today's instant world of dopamine whenever we want it, patience often becomes a forgotten virtue, but in order to feel joy in the godly we need patience.

Parables, Prompt 2

Please answer the following questions in your scripture journal: Which characters in the parable do you identify with most and why? Can you see any particular ways you might personally apply what you learned in this parable?

I identify a bit with the laborers that were there the whole day. I have grown up in the Church and done all the things like a mission, going to church, etc. As a kid, I once heard a story of a man that got baptized when he was in his old age, a man that died only a short time after. I remember feeling jealous and having wished that I had waited to be baptized when I was about to die so that I could know I would be pure. I now realize how wrong I was as I look back at how the gospel and my desire and effort to be diligent in keeping God's commandments has blessed my life. I realize that I am much happier and more joyful because of my baptism and the gift of the Holy Ghost in my life, and I recognize that as I strive to keep my covenants and repent, I can retain the remission of my sins as I take the sacrament. During my mission I had the opportunity to be guided by the Spirit to teach a man who had a dangerous health condition. Each Sunday, he walked to church a rather far distance even with the pain that his condition caused to his leg. He had so much faith. I could tell that, like the 11th-hour laborers, the Lord had been preparing him throughout his life. He was baptized and within a month he passed away. I can remember a much different feeling when I heard this. Rather than feeling envious knowing he was clean and prepared to meet God, I felt sad to hear of his passing, but I also felt an overwhelming joy for him and his decision to join the vineyard during the “final hour.” I can apply these teachings by always remembering this experience and this man if I ever experience envy regarding others.

Parables, Prompt 3

Please answer the following questions in your scripture journal: What stands out to you from the parable of the unmerciful servant? Can you see any particular ways you might personally apply what you learned in this parable?

What stands out to me is how quickly he went to seek his own debt. It also stood out to me that he lacked compassion even when the man that owed him pleaded for forgiveness in the exact same verbiage. I can apply this by forgiving others in my life, due to my remembrance that I depend on God's mercy.

Parables, Prompt 4

Please answer the following questions in your scripture journal: What stands out to you from these parables? Can you see any particular ways you might personally apply what you learned in these parables?

What stands out is the focus on how we need to prioritize in our lives. The Lord wants us to put Him first. In so doing, He will help us accomplish the rest. He won't let us go about this life alone. Likewise, we need not focus on worldly things and we must impart of our substance one with another and share and serve and give unto the less fortunate.

Parables, Prompt 5

As you review the parables of the Good Samaritan and Prodigal Son, what do you think these parables can do for you today, right now in your life?

These parables remind me of the quickness and readiness of God's forgiveness. They tell me that God is ready to forgive and that our repentance is dependent completely on us and when we decide to turn to Him. I can apply this by repenting daily. I can also apply the teachings of the Good Samaritan by letting go of my pride and prejudice and loving everyone in my deeds and actions.

Miracles, Prompt 1

In your scripture journal, please answer two of the following questions: Of all the miracles you read about in Matthew 8-9, which one or two impressed you the most? What evidence of faith in Jesus Christ did you find in the miracles you read about? What might you do to better show faith in Christ and have greater gratitude for the Lord's miracles in your life?

The story that was most striking to me was that of the Centurion. I imagine this powerful man who is the leader of 100 soldiers. Add him being a Roman and one might think him to be the last person to have the humility to have faith in the Savior. All facts about him make me imagine him to be this prideful man, yet he had greater faith than all of Israel. This story was a testament to me that regardless of where you are or what assumptions people make about you, or how much success you have, you can be a faithful and humble saint like that centurion. You can study your scriptures the same way he knew Jewish law despite being Roman, and your righteous desires (like how he wanted his servant healed) can be realized through Christ and His miracles. I can stand in holy places, testify of Christ, and maintain my own integrity and develop more humility in order to emulate this type of faith. I can also look for miracles and acknowledge them and thank the Lord in gratitude each day.

Miracles, Prompt 2

In your scripture journal, please choose one of the miracles you read about in the Gospel of John and answer both of the following questions: Why do you think John included this miracle in his Gospel? How would you describe the Savior's relationships with the other people involved in the story?

John's focus was largely in demonstrating Christ's divinity and His place in the Godhead. Describing this miracle of Christ bringing a man that was dead four days back to life demonstrates godly power that no man could fake. It shows that He is greater than all the prophets and truly is a member of the Godhead that was sent by God and has His power and authority. This story also mentions explicitly Christ's love for Lazarus and how He is willing to weep and mourn with those that mourn as He taught in the Sermon on the Mount, demonstrating that He is perfect and possesses each of the attributes He described.

Christ's relationship with the other people involved in this story is deeply intimate. He is mentioned crying with them and loving Lazarus. This is one of the scriptures that most demonstrates Christ's love, personal connections, and emotion as He interacts with this beloved family. Christ cares about people and can be recognized literally bearing their pains, afflictions, and burdens, crying while knowing full well that Lazarus is soon to be brought back. Just His understanding of the pain they felt thinking He would be gone forever was enough to cause Jesus to weep.

Miracles, Prompt 3

In your scripture journal, please answer the following questions: As you have studied some of the miracles of Christ in this lesson, what did you notice about His character? How would you describe Christ based on the scripture stories you have read for this lesson? What do these miracles mean for you in your life today?

The most notable thing that I noticed about Christ's character during this lesson is His mercy. I almost think of Him like a famous athlete or musician that people always beg for an autograph from. I'd imagine that they eventually get annoyed and don't want to autograph anymore, but Jesus, He just performed one miracle after another and performed miracles on the way to do other ones. He never grew tired of serving and loving with mercy and compassion. These miracles provide me with a hope and faith that Christ can perform miracles, physical, psychological, and especially spiritual, in my own life if I simply believe and thank Him.
