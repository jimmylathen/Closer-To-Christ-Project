Unit 8

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Although Christ is our eternal judge, in His earthly ministry He was not a 'judging' individual. He treated the most marginalized and sinful individuals with respect and love. If the all-powerful Son of God has such humility, then as an imperfect person, I must do the same. I must reach out with love and compassion to others, withholding all prejudice."

Context Summary

Lesson 14 examined the Savior's compassion and forgiveness as shown in Luke's Gospel, focusing on His ministry to the poor, overlooked, and sorrowing, and His willingness to forgive even those who had sinned greatly. Through stories such as Mary's Magnificat, Christ's teachings on wealth and the poor, and the account of a sinful woman anointing His feet in a Pharisee's house, the lesson showed how Jesus broke social and religious boundaries to minister to those on the margins of society.

Full Journal Submission

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 1

What stands out to you about the way Mary describes herself? What do you notice about the blessings God will bring to her and all of Israel?

Mary describes herself with humility, being one of the lowest of low. She is grateful for the condescension of God and His choosing her. God is bringing blessings to her by having her son bless all of Israel by being their Savior. She is blessed as all will remember her and hold her to the highest esteem.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 2

What do you make of all of these teachings? In today's society, there is a heavy emphasis on material possessions. What do you think Jesus would say to people in first-world countries about wealth if He were here today?

What I make of this is priority. Christ shows that we must prioritize and love God above all else. In so doing, we will be willing to impart our possessions and wealth so as to bless the lives of others, especially the ostracized of society. Jesus would likely tell us to prioritize the kingdom of God and not let temporal things take the forefront of our mind, energy, and cares. He would also want us to give to those in need.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 3

Who are the “widows” or the socially, economically, or vulnerable people in your circle? What would it look like in your life to fulfill your covenantal obligation to go out of your way to minister to them? How would those actions represent you “preaching the good news to the poor”?

The “widows” in my circle are not just the literal widows in my ward. They include those that are visiting, or simply seem lonely or left out, those that may call themselves misfits. Christ invites all to come unto Him. All fit into His gospel. If I were to minister to them I would spend time with them to offer friendship, understand their needs, and try to offer assistance in every way that I can. Thus I would preach the good news to the poor by offering hope.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 4

What does the story tell us about the owner of the house? What do you learn about Jesus's compassion for the owner?

This tells us that the owner of the house was a Pharisee. Men that had rejected and were doubting Christ. Furthermore, he asks how Jesus does not realize she's a sinner, challenging His authority. Jesus, knowing this, showed compassion by entering his house and ministering to him individually. He also reproved him in a kind way and implied that he as well may be forgiven for his few sins, yet would not love the Lord as much.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 5

What does the story tell us about the woman? What do you learn about Jesus's compassion for her?

The woman was a sinner, likely a sinner that had already been forgiven by Jesus before. It tells us that she is faithful and showing gratitude and love for the Savior for His mercy on her. He loves her and recognizes her love for Him.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 6

Take a minute to reflect on what you wrote in Prompt 5 about Jesus's compassion for the woman. Now that we've talked about the importance of you identifying with the woman and seeing yourself in the story, reflect on your experiences with Jesus's compassion and about receiving forgiveness from and through Him.

Many times, in moments where I was living very imperfectly and felt guilt, I would still have profound spiritual experiences when I made an effort. I recognized that that meant the Atonement was working in my life. I didn't feel like I deserved that kind of mercy and it brought me nearly to tears when it happened. I am eternally grateful for His longsuffering patience and mercy with me.

The Compassionate and Forgiving Jesus, Prompt 7

Take a few minutes to write about the most important principle that you have learned from this class and consider at least one way that you can respond with greater compassion to those who are on the periphery of your circles of influence.

The most important principles that I have learned are about, first, the way in which Christ loved and ministered to the one. The lesson I am learning is that each thing Christ asks of us, He did and still does to perfection. I know that when I struggle to show mercy, I can appreciate the way in which Christ has perfected that. I love to see the way He showed mercy to the marginalized. The other most important takeaway was that I need to do my best to emulate the way in which the Savior did so. As I strive, I can feel His mercy and have charity and view others as He does.
