Unit 10

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Being a powerful leader means knowing when to use that power and having the self-control not to when it is not expedient to do so. Christ is the perfect example of this during His suffering on the cross. The living Christ always refers to His crucifixion, and thus we need to maintain an emphasis on His physical sacrifice when we refer to Christ's Atonement."

Context Summary

Lesson 17 examined Christ's trial before Pilate, His crucifixion, and burial through the accounts of the Gospels, along with historical background on the practice of crucifixion and how John uniquely connects Christ's death to the Passover. Lesson 18 explored Christ's spirit-world ministry and His Resurrection, comparing how the Gospels describe His appearances to His disciples as the risen Lord and what those appearances teach about the nature of the Resurrection and the living Christ. Together, these lessons trace the arc from the cross to the empty tomb and call each reader to renewed discipleship.

Full Journal Submission

Crucifixion, Prompt 1

Scourging was a standard Roman practice and had been required by Pilate. The mocking was, however, not required. What can we learn from the episode of the mockery and abuse that Christ experienced from the Romans? What symbolism does the crown of thorns suggest to you?

When I imagine Christ's power and His ability to have destroyed those that were harming Him, I come to understand Christ's love and character so much more. Not only did He suffer, but He demonstrated incredible self-control. Even understanding His mission and what He was doing, it seems nearly impossible in the moment to not decide to free yourself of that pain. Surely any other crucified person, having Christ's power, would have used it to be freed, no matter how strong of will they may have. The mockery and symbolism of the crown of thorns, as Christ truly is king and will wear a crown, is striking. The mockery and satire is blasphemy and crude. The fact that they enjoyed it and laughed at such a gory and disturbing scene makes it hard for me to feel the Savior's love for them, but Christ still loved them, did not lash out, or use His power.

Crucifixion, Prompt 2

What's different in John versus the synoptic Gospels?

In John, the author states that Christ carried His cross alone.

Crucifixion, Prompt 3

What detail does Luke add that is missing from the others?

That Christ tells the women not to weep for Him but for themselves.

Crucifixion, Prompt 4

How do these help us see the unique portraits given of the Savior? Compare Lesson 9, “Portraits of Jesus and Christology in the Gospels.”

High Christology in John demonstrates His strength while being alone in the burden of carrying the cross, whereas the story Luke tells shows His empathy and compassion. During this excruciating pain, when someone mortal would have no capability of thinking of anyone but themselves, Christ thinks of the women, the marginalized of society.

Crucifixion, Prompt 5

Briefly share what you learned and felt about the Crucifixion after reading this chapter.

What I learned from this chapter was that crucifixion was not a rare occurrence but a real and rather common practice in Jesus' time. I also learned that crucifixion was much broader than we normally realize and that not all crucifixions looked like Christ's. Furthermore, I discovered that many Jews and Christians were very uncomfortable with or even mocked the idea that Christ died on the cross.

Crucifixion, Prompt 6

Describe four ways that John connects Christ's Crucifixion with the Passover.

John connects the Crucifixion with the Passover because Christ was killed yet none of His bones were broken. Furthermore, Christ was given hyssop just like the lambs in the Passover. Furthermore, He was called the Lamb of God. And lastly, His crucifixion occurred during the actual Passover.

Crucifixion, Prompt 7

Identify the seven final statements of Jesus Christ on the cross.

The seven final statements of Jesus Christ on the cross are as follows: “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing. Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise. Father, into your hands I commend my spirit. Woman, here is your son. Here is your mother. I am thirsty. It is finished.”

Resurrection, Prompt 1

In your scripture journal, please answer the following questions: In all the accounts of the Savior's Resurrection and His interactions with His Apostles and disciples, which story or interaction had the greatest impact on you, and why? How might a knowledge of the Savior's Resurrection and your future resurrection influence your perspective of mortal life? What statement from Elder Holland's talk helps John 21 become more applicable for you personally?

The story of the Resurrection that John tells has the most impact on me. Particularly his interaction with Mary Magdalene. I loved the way in which the Lord was patient with her and calm as He spoke to her according to her own understanding, as if He really were a gardener. This interaction reminds me that God always taught us through our lens and understanding. Not everyone got the same answer right as they asked it. God knows what we can handle and treats us accordingly. However, when Mary was ready, He called her by name. I often think of Christ calling me by name and talking to me as I view His resurrected glory. This must have been an incredible moment in Mary's life.

The knowledge of the Savior's Resurrection has a profound impact on my life. For one, because He rose again, I know that I will too receive a resurrected body. This helps me to have hope and courage, not needing to fear death. Likewise, I know that even if I lose members of my family during this mortal journey, they will live on, and if I live worthily I will live with them for all of eternity. This is encouraging and helps reduce anxiety regarding my siblings and parents, my wife, and my future children. Furthermore, understanding the Resurrection allows me to foster an eternal perspective and think celestial.

Knowing that my actions here matter for eternity causes me to open my mind and prioritize things of eternal importance over the more trivial day-to-day matters of my life. When making decisions I can think less about instant pleasure and gratification and focus on the enduring implications of my actions. As I think celestial, I can remember Jesus Christ in everyday situations, which allows me to better treat those around me and give value to scripture study and service. This leads to better keeping the first and second great commandments to love the Lord and my neighbor.

This talk by Elder Holland titled “The First Great Commandment” is one of my favorites. During my mission I listened to the MTC talk he gave with this title rather frequently and even created a training based on it. It inspired me throughout my mission and allowed me to deepen my conversion and commitment to the Lord. Now it provides a message that may be even more applicable in my life. I gave my whole soul during my missionary service. As I listen to this talk, I wonder if I am continuing that conversion and making it deeper and stronger, or if I have “returned to the boats and the fish.” It allows me to reflect on my life and actions and how I am changing and realign my priorities.

Clearly, I am no longer a full-time missionary, but this class has taught me that there is a lot more to being a disciple than knocking on doors and talking to strangers. I can look outward to others and upward to God in my day-to-day interactions and find ways to serve and love and treat people as Christ would. I can study to deepen my faith and share what I have learned with those around me that I love. I can pray more earnestly and choose the right. If Peter had an opportunity to repent and recommit himself to the mission he served for three years with the Lord by his side, so can I.
