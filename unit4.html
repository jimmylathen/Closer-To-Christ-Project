Unit 4

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Christ was brave. He pushed all cultural boundaries courageously because of what He knew was right. He did not let His fear of men inhibit Him from accomplishing the will of the Father, and I must likewise strive to do the same."

Context Summary

Lessons 7 and 8 examined the political, cultural, and religious landscape of first-century Palestine to provide context for Jesus's ministry. Lesson 7 covered the political development from the Persian through Roman periods, explaining the social dynamics of Galilee, Judea, and the Jewish community. Lesson 8 surveyed Jewish sectarian groups, including the Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, and Zealots, showing how their differing beliefs about God, scripture, and righteousness shaped the way people responded to Jesus, and how the messianic expectations of the day differed significantly from what Christ actually accomplished.

Jesus in Historical Context I, Prompt 1

How has knowing the historical context of a specific scripture passage, one of the texts Dr. Grey discussed, or another text of your choice, aided you in understanding the text?

In Mosiah, understanding the context of the people that were slaves that could not pray to God without being killed helped me understand their situation and feel for them. Then as I read about the way God had mercy on them and carried them through their struggles and helped ease their burdens until ultimately freeing them helped me understand it too. But knowing even more context as I read the Book of Mormon from cover to cover and seeing that this occurred shortly after their baptism in the waters of Mormon made me understand even more. It helped me see more of how and why God allows bad things to happen to good people that are striving to do what's right. Then, applying it to myself made this passage of scripture all the more powerful.

Jesus in Historical Context I, Prompt 2

Now that you have learned about the historical, political, and cultural landscape of first-century Palestine, briefly write some personal reflections on how this information clarifies aspects of Jesus's life, teachings, or ministry that inspire you, and how you think that understanding historical context could help you find more meaningful personal application in your scripture study.

Having learned about the historical, political, and cultural landscape of first-century Palestine, I have some personal reflections on how this information clarifies aspects of Jesus's life, teachings, and ministry in ways that inspire me and make me think that understanding historical context could help me find more meaningful personal application in my scripture study. For one, I didn't really understand that Sadducees and Pharisees were different sects that had some tension within themselves. I kind of just grouped them together as a self-righteous bunch of Jesus persecutors. Understanding their differences and perspectives provides more context to apply in my study in seeing how they question Jesus and how Jesus chooses to respond. Furthermore, I always knew that Jesus was of humble beginnings. However, understanding how the settlement in Galilee came to be and figuring out the way in which He was able to relate to other poor country people brings greater light to this fact. Ultimately, understanding the social dynamic in ancient Israel gives a lot of perspective to help understand the tensions and interactions within different social classes.

Jesus in Historical Context II, Prompt 1

Briefly write some personal reflections on how learning about Jewish sectarian groups helps you find deeper meaning in the teachings of Jesus, how you see some tendencies of these groups within our own community, and (based on His interactions in the first century) how you think Jesus might respond to these tendencies in our own day.

I thought it was super interesting to learn about Jewish sects. I realized why Jesus rebuked Sadducees and Pharisees in different ways. I got to understand why zealots revolted. I discovered more about the Essenes and their similarities and differences with Christianity and particularly Latter-day Saints. It was interesting to see how Christ brought the focus of Jews with different perspectives back to where it was supposed to be, with the proper doctrine and understanding, taking away distracting strict laws that prejudiced the poor and ill. I think Christ today might correct some of our cultural judgments or even policies we have today that are imperfect applications of His divine commandments. For example, we have ideas about what people should or shouldn't wear or do to their bodies that are cultural rather than doctrinal in the Church that could make people feel excluded. I think Christ would go to those people with love and help them.

Jesus in Historical Context II, Prompt 2

What do these passages say about the nature and mission of this messianic king, and what do they not say? If all you had access to were these prophetic writings, what would you expect the messiah to accomplish?

The messianic expectations were both fulfilled and unmet by Christ's visitation during the meridian of time. The people expected Christ to come from the clouds with glory and power, kill the children of Sheth, and bring mass destruction upon the wicked, earning the Israelites all their land. They expected the lion to lie with the kid and for there to be peace, the gathering of the lost tribes, and four great beasts to emerge. On the other hand, Christ's earthly ministry did fulfill some expectations. A star appeared, Christ is a righteous and merciful judge to the poor and weak, and He did smite the Earth with the rod of His mouth, figuratively killing the wicked with His words.

Jesus in Historical Context II, Prompt 3

Based on contemporary expectations of what the messiah would accomplish, why would even Jesus's earliest disciples not have clearly understood His mission to suffer and die on a cross?

The disciples, after having read the Torah, likely expected Christ to fight back against the Pharisees, Sadducees, and Romans that sought to destroy Him, destroying them and providing land for the true Israelites as prophecy claimed He would. The idea that He Himself would be meek and be killed for our sins would not have crossed their minds, though it is clear to us now. They thought Jesus would be a literal king and He had to run away to avoid being made so. They did not understand the resurrection after three days as well, nor the concept that there would be a Second Coming, so much so that they did not originally fully believe Christ had risen.

Jesus in Historical Context II, Prompt 4

What do these Book of Mormon passages say about the nature and mission of the messiah, and how does this clarity differ from the Old Testament prophetic passages? What insights about the nature of Christ did the Nephites have that the ancient Jewish community apparently did not?

These Book of Mormon passages demonstrate how the Nephites received much more specific witnesses and prophecies of Jesus Christ before His coming. The Book of Mormon prophets declared Christ's name, mother, and earthly mission and ministry in detail before His coming. They explained how He would atone for our sins and be our Savior. They testified of His crucifixion and resurrection after three days. The prophecies were clear and fully fulfilled, unlike the Old Testament prophecies that only now make sense to us knowing what Christ already has done and what He will do at the Second Coming.
