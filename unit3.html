Unit 3

Coming Closer to Christ

What I Want to Remember from This Unit:

"Christ is and always has been the light of the world. The Law of Moses truly was preparatory to His coming and was symbolic of Him and His Atonement."

Context Summary

Lesson 5 explored the Law of Moses as a divinely revealed system pointing forward to Jesus Christ, examining how its ordinances, sacrifices, feasts, and symbols prefigured His life and mission. Lesson 6 turned to Isaiah's far-reaching prophecies about the Savior, showing how his words speak across multiple historical settings and reveal Christ as Jehovah, Redeemer, and King. Together, the lessons demonstrated that both the Law and the prophets were always testifying of the same person and the same saving mission.

Full Journal Submission

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 1

The ancient Israelites offered animal sacrifices but did not have the ordinance of the sacrament. You partake of the sacrament but do not offer animal sacrifices. In your journal, write down as many purposes of sacrifice as you can remember, and then consider whether any of them might reflect an eternal principle that could be applicable to your sacrament experience.

In ancient Israel, sacrifices were performed for a variety of reasons. For one, sacrifices were made to make atonement, which to them meant to reconcile with God. Sacrifices were also made as a sacrifice for sin. Lastly, sacrifices could be done voluntarily as thanksgiving. When I partake of the sacrament I can pay attention to the way Christ has been that sacrifice for me each week. I can recognize the grace that the Atonement of Christ has brought me each week, I can take the sacrament to cleanse my sins each week, and I can partake of the sacrament to show my gratitude to God.

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 2

How does Lehi connect this type of animal sacrifice to Jesus Christ? Explain Lehi's explanation as best you can in your own words.

Lehi ties sin offerings to Jesus Christ by describing how He offers Himself as a sacrifice for sin. He describes that as long as we sacrifice a broken heart and contrite spirit, we can be cleansed by His sin offering.

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 3

Write down a few ways in which this description of the Passover lamb reminds you about Jesus. Compare your ideas with John 1:29, 36; 19:29, 32, 36; 20:31.

These passages of scripture compare Christ to the Passover. John calls Christ the Lamb of God, referring to the Passover: as the Israelites had been saved by the lamb's blood, so too shall we be saved by the blood of Christ. The scriptures reference that Christ's bones were not broken, similar to the lamb at Passover.

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 4

Against the backdrop of the huge lights dramatically illuminating the temple grounds, Jesus testified, “I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life” (John 8:12). In your journal, write what you think this means. How do these images of light and darkness relate to your own experience with the Savior?

During the times in my life that I have felt close to the Savior, I have felt His light. I have even had the opportunity to feel His light radiate off of me. When my priorities have shifted, I have felt a darkness that I have sometimes blamed on other things. However, ultimately, the darkness is because of a loss of identity and prioritization of Christ. Christ is the light of my life and He who brings me joy.

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 5

After declaring “I am the light of the world” a second time (John 9:5), Jesus anoints the blind man's eyes and tells him to wash in the pool of Siloam. How do (1) Jesus's testimony of light and (2) His instructions about the water from Siloam relate to the Feast of Tabernacles?

Jesus' testimony of light relates to the Feast of Tabernacles because Menorah lamps were lit symbolically during the feast. The feast was also done using water to pray for the rain for the next year. Christ saying He is the living water would also thus have deep symbolic meaning.

Christ and the Law of Moses, Prompt 6

The first time the formerly blind man describes Jesus (John 9:11), what does he call Him? The second time (John 9:17), what does he call Him? The third time (John 9:38), what does he call Him? What do you make of this progression? Is there another kind of blindness that this man is overcoming?

The first time, he simply refers to Christ as a man called Jesus. However, throughout the process, the blind man is overcoming more than just physical blindness but also spiritual blindness. He later, after exercising his faith, refers to Christ as a “prophet,” thus demonstrating his greater perspective and deeper understanding of the Son of God.

Isaiah, Prompt 1

What can we learn about Jesus Christ from this passage? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

From this passage we can learn a lot about Jesus. We can learn that He works through prophets. We can also learn that He gets offended when we refuse to listen to them, as they are His voice. We can also learn that the Lord will cause negative consequences when we disobey so that we might recognize our sins, humble ourselves, return to Him, and repent.

Isaiah, Prompt 2

If you interpret Isaiah 5:26-30 as Jesus Christ raising an ensign to gather His people in the last days, what does that teach you about the Savior's attributes or mission? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

Reading that passage as Christ being an ensign to all nations brings a different perspective to Christ's power and love. It shows that Christ will gather Israel and that His mission really is to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man. It demonstrates His power to use imperfect individuals through missionary work in gathering His Saints from abroad.

Isaiah, Prompt 3

What can we learn about Jesus Christ from the way He responds to Ahaz through the prophet Isaiah? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

We can learn that Christ is willing to show His power through signs and wonders. We learn that Christ has the right path because of His infinite knowledge, even when His ways don't appear to be the most logical path. We can learn that Christ is merciful and patient with unbelievers and provides them with knowledge so that they may choose for themselves.

Isaiah, Prompt 4

If you interpret Isaiah 7:14 as a prophecy of the birth of Jesus Christ in the meridian of time, what does that teach you about the Savior's attributes or mission? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

We also can connect this prophecy to the birth of Christ through the Virgin Mary. Thus, it teaches of Christ's perfection, even when He was young and unlearned. It also shows that future prophets can recognize and testify of multiple fulfillings of the same prophecy. It shows that that verse was not meant to only have one meaning, but an all-knowing God that knows the end from the beginning can inspire prophecy before the prophet themselves even understands all the applications.

Isaiah, Prompt 5

What can we learn about Jesus Christ from this passage? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

This passage teaches of Christ's mercy for Israel. Despite the consequences that occurred due to their lack of obedience, in due time Christ prepared a way for their salvation. The Persians would be much nicer to the Israelites and give them their land back. The wasteland that the Babylonians had made would be restored due to the Persians. They were saved by a longsuffering God.

Isaiah, Prompt 6

As you read, jot down ways in which you observe Abinadi interpreting the words of Isaiah 52:7-10. Pay particular attention to how Abinadi uses the words redeem/redemption and salvation. When you have read Mosiah 13:33-16:12 and finished making your observations, compare what you have found with the suggestions below.

Abinadi starts by framing this in terms of the final judgment and what happens to us when we die in our sins. He distinguishes between the Lord's people and sinners that die in their sins, saying the Lord's people will be redeemed and the sinners cannot be. He says that the Lord will provide salvation to His people and that the prophets are those that bear good tidings to the righteous and bad tidings to the wicked.

Isaiah, Prompt 7

If you interpret Isaiah 52:7-10 as a prophecy of Jesus's atoning sacrifice in the meridian of time, what does that teach you about the Savior's attributes or mission? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

This interpretation teaches so much about Christ's mercy and Atonement. It shares that He is our Savior, and willing to bless and redeem each one of us, bringing us to salvation.

Isaiah, Prompt 8

If you interpret Isaiah 52:7-10 as Jesus Christ blessing and protecting scattered Israel in the last days, what does that teach you about the Savior's attributes or mission? Write down as many ideas as you can think of.

If you interpret the passage as Christ blessing and protecting scattered Israel in the last days, it teaches that Christ loves us and wants to gather His sheep in this day and age and that at the Second Coming He will gather and bless them.

Isaiah, Prompt 9

Now that you've read Isaiah 54 and seen a quick summary of its original context, practice likening it in several different ways. For each of the following settings, look through Isaiah 54 and find lines or phrases that can be likened in meaningful ways to that setting.

Setting 1: Before the coming of Christ in 3 Nephi, there had been destruction, lamentation, earthquakes, and darkness. The righteous also experienced all of this misery and were mourning their lost family. They likely felt that the Lord had abandoned them. However, this message of peace would have meant everything to them. They would have felt the love of the Lord from the Savior Himself in saying that He would comfort and be there for them, to never forsake them.

Setting 2: Latter-day Saints have also been blessed in the ways the Lord described. Through their persecutions and travels to the West, we all know they suffered great hardships. Family members died and left the Church and they likely felt the Lord had abandoned them despite sacrificing everything for His restored gospel. Nonetheless, this promise describes the way in which the Lord has now blessed us to overcome many of these challenges. This will further be fulfilled at the Second Coming. The Lord has been merciful and loving to His people in the latter days.

Setting 3: The phrase, “For a small moment have I forsaken thee, but with great mercies will I gather thee,” is particularly meaningful to me. I would not say that the Lord has ever forsaken me. Honestly it is more like at times I have forsaken Him, as I know He is always reaching out to me. However, I do recognize the ways in which the Lord has sought after and gathered me, despite my imperfections, constantly beckoning me to return to the path and do what is right. I have seen His longsuffering mercy on me. The line “the mountains shall depart and the hills be removed, but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the Lord that hath mercy on thee” is also meaningful to me as I recognize that the Lord is merciful with me in my weaker moments and my mistakes.
